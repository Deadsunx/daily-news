## 1. Gunmen Kill at Least 27 in Two Mass Shootings Across South Africa
*World & Geopolitics · CBS News (AFP)*

Two separate mass shootings in South Africa over the weekend left at least 27 people dead and dozens wounded, police said. In the Johannesburg area, eight gunmen with assault rifles stormed a bar on Saturday evening, killing 17 and wounding 15; in a Cape Town-area township early Sunday, gunmen attacked an entertainment venue, killing 10 and wounding 11. No suspects have been arrested and a motive for the Johannesburg attack remains unknown. The attacks add to a persistent wave of gun violence in South Africa, which officials say averages roughly 60 murders a day nationally, often linked to gang rivalries.

## 2. Israel to Revoke Credentials of Dutch Diplomats in Ramallah Over Settlement Trade Ban
*World & Geopolitics · The Times of Israel*

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar announced that Israel will revoke diplomatic credentials and privileges for Dutch diplomats posted in Ramallah within seven days. The move responds to a Dutch ban, which took effect the same day, on importing goods from Israeli businesses in the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and the Golan Heights. Sa'ar said the diplomats could continue representing Dutch interests with the Palestinians from Palestinian Authority territory rather than Israeli soil, and warned that countries acting in a "hostile and unilateral manner" would lose influence in the region. Israel had already barred Dutch officials from a Gaza aid coordination center the previous month over the same trade policy.

## 3. US and China Agree to Cut Tariffs on $30 Billion in Goods, Launch AI Dialogue
*Business & Markets · Reuters (via GV Wire)*

Following President Xi Jinping's three-day visit to Washington that ended Friday, the United States and China agreed to reduce tariffs on $30 billion worth of goods traded in each direction, the White House said. The tariff relief covers US exports such as agricultural products, wood, and cosmetics, and Chinese-made goods including small appliances, toys, and decorations; the two countries also agreed to establish a joint trade council and extend an earlier tariff truce by two months. Beijing and Washington additionally agreed to hold a dialogue on AI risks and benefits, with a first session set for November and a new incident-communication channel. Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi called the visit a step that "opened a new chapter" in bilateral relations.

## 4. China Signals It May Clear Alibaba, ByteDance to Buy New Nvidia AI Chips
*AI & Tech · Bloomberg / Seeking Alpha*

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology has asked Alibaba and ByteDance for details on their planned purchases of Nvidia's new RTX Pro 5500 chip, including expected order volumes and intended use, according to The Information as reported by Bloomberg. A recent US easing of some restrictions on Nvidia products bound for China has so far involved mostly older-generation chips in relatively small volumes.

## 5. Meta Patches Security Flaw in Muse AI Agent That Exposed User Data
*AI & Tech · Reuters (via KSL.com)*

Meta has fixed a security vulnerability in its Muse AI agent that could have let an attacker access a user's dedicated virtual machine, including emails and files, according to Reuters, citing The Information. Meta classified the flaw as "SEV-2," its third-highest internal severity rating, and the issue was reported through the company's bug bounty program by an outside researcher rather than found in active use. In response, Meta is adding a clearer in-app safety warning to Muse; the company did not respond to Reuters' request for comment. Muse launched earlier in September as Meta's personal AI agent for tasks like shopping, travel booking, and email, and had drawn about 2.8 million downloads in its first two weeks, according to Sensor Tower.

## 6. CERN Begins Dismantling Large Hadron Collider Magnets for Major Upgrade
*Science · CERN (via ScienceDaily)*

CERN has begun disconnecting sections of the 27-kilometer Large Hadron Collider on either side of the ATLAS and CMS experiments, starting the process of replacing 28 superconducting magnets as part of the High-Luminosity LHC upgrade. The current niobium-titanium magnets, installed between 2005 and 2007, are being replaced with new niobium-tin magnets that generate an 11.3 tesla field, about 40% stronger, to allow tighter beam compression and more particle collisions for analysis. Dismantling began September 7 during the collider's third long shutdown; CERN's Jean-Philippe Tock said the first new quadrupole magnet is expected to arrive in the tunnel in early 2029. In total, 16 cryostats and 28 cryo-assemblies will be installed as part of the project.
