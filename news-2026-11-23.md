## 1. COP31 climate summit in Antalya ends without an agreement
*World & Geopolitics · CBS News*

The UN COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, ended on Sunday without a final agreement after running into overtime, following two weeks of negotiations among delegations from nearly 200 countries. The talks broke down mainly over fossil fuels: a coalition led by France and Panama, with countries from Europe, Africa, Latin America and the Pacific, rejected the latest draft as too weak because it contained no fossil fuel language. An earlier draft had called for accelerated action to phase out fossil fuels, but that wording was removed days later. The United States sent no representatives for the second year in a row following its withdrawal from the Paris Agreement.

## 3. Lean and Mathlib communities say machine-checked proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct
*Science · Lean Community Blog*

The Lean and Mathlib communities issued a joint statement, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, saying they have "full confidence" that a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis released by mathematician Levent Alpöge is correct. Alpöge released the proof on November 20 as a Lean library of about 17 million lines of code. According to the statement, the proof was checked by several independent proof checkers, including Lean's kernel, and tools confirmed that the proved theorem matches Mathlib's formal statement of the Riemann Hypothesis.

## 4. Signal restricts screenshots on Android and iOS, citing AI agents as an 'existential threat'
*AI & Tech · TechCrunch*

Signal announced it is restricting screenshots in its messaging app on Android and iOS, extending a protection that was already on by default on Windows. Signal President Meredith Whittaker said agentic AI built into operating systems is an "existential threat" and that Windows, Android and macOS/iOS "are undermining Signal's ability to independently guarantee privacy." Users can turn the setting off, though Whittaker said doing so would "break our privacy guarantees" and described the fix as "incomplete."

## 6. Relativistic effects rewrite the textbook triple bond in a carbon-bismuth molecule
*Science · ScienceDaily (Brown University)*

Researchers led by Lai-Sheng Wang at Brown University reported in Science that the triple bond in the carbon-bismuth molecular ion CBi- does not follow the textbook model of one sigma bond plus two pi bonds. Using photoelectron spectroscopy on molecules cooled to near absolute zero, the team found the bond consists of one pi bond and two hybrid sigma-pi bonds. The authors attribute the change to relativistic spin-orbit coupling in the heavy bismuth atom, showing how Einstein's relativity can alter basic chemical bonding rules for heavy elements.
