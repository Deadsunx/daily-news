# Daily News — 2026-08-20

## 1. UAE Imposes Indefinite Trade Embargo on Iran After Missile Accusation
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

The United Arab Emirates halted all trade, commercial exchanges and financial transactions with Iran on August 19, after accusing Iranian forces of firing two ballistic missiles toward UAE waters, one of which landed within its territorial waters. Iran's foreign ministry denied the claim, calling it a "false flag operation" and urging regional restraint. The alleged strike would be the first targeting the UAE since May and comes a day after a 60-day US-Iran negotiating window expired without a breakthrough. Analysts say the embargo could hit Iran harder than US sanctions, since Dubai has become Iran's largest single trading partner, supplying roughly a third of its annual imports.

## 2. Trump Pauses 50% Tariffs on Canada After Last-Minute Trade Deal
*Business & Markets · NPR (via The Associated Press)*

President Trump delayed 50% tariffs on $20 billion worth of Canadian imports for three days after Canada and the US reached a last-minute deal less than two hours before the tariffs were due to take effect at midnight on August 19. The White House said Canada had committed to removing measures it considers discriminatory against US alcohol, dairy and auto exports, though Canada did not immediately confirm those terms. Prime Minister Mark Carney said "substantial progress" had been made but that important work remained, with the pause running until August 21. The two countries exchanged $880 billion in goods and services last year, and nearly 72% of Canada's exports go to the US.

[Trump says U.S. and Canada reached deal to delay 50% U.S. tariffs on Canadian imports](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/19/g-s1-139156/trump-canada-tariffs)

## 3. Landmark Trial Opens Over Meta's Alleged Design to Addict Children
*Business & Markets · NPR*

A trial pitting four states, including California, Colorado and Kentucky, against Meta began in federal court, with the states alleging the company knowingly designed Facebook and Instagram to hook young users and misled the public about the harm. In opening statements, a California deputy attorney general said Meta's business model can be summed up as "hook," "hold," "harvest" and "hide." Potential penalties in the case have been estimated as high as $1.4 trillion, roughly equal to Meta's entire Nasdaq market value. Legal experts have compared the case to 1990s tobacco litigation that forced sweeping changes on that industry.

## 4. OpenAI Pauses Frontier AI Training Run Over Cyber Risk Concerns
*AI & Tech · TechCrunch*

OpenAI said it paused reinforcement-learning training for two weeks and rolled out new monitoring safeguards after a security incident involving Hugging Face and preliminary signs that its upcoming Astra model could meet the "Critical" cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework. The company's largest planned frontier training run remains on hold while it hardens research environments and runs smaller-scale evaluations instead. New activation classifiers now inspect model activity token-by-token and escalate suspicious behavior, such as unauthorized access or attempts to bypass safeguards, to automated investigators within roughly 30 minutes. OpenAI also began testing "Private Safety Processing," a feature meant to deter hacking attempts by both humans and misaligned AI agents.

[OpenAI institutes new safeguards after Hugging Face breach](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/openai-institutes-new-safeguards-after-hugging-face-breach/)

## 5. Astronomers Identify Fastest Star Ever Observed, Orbiting Milky Way's Black Hole
*Science · NPR*

Researchers led by Stefan Gillessen at the Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics identified a star called S301 that reaches speeds over 15,000 miles per second, nearly 8% of the speed of light, at its closest approach to Sagittarius A*, the supermassive black hole at the Milky Way's center. The findings, published in the journal Nature on August 19, make S301 the fastest star ever observed in the galaxy, completing an elongated 8.7-year orbit around the black hole. Because the star passes so close to Sagittarius A*, scientists say it can be used to measure how the black hole's gravity distorts space and time, and potentially determine how fast the black hole is spinning. Outside researchers called the result a step toward unprecedented measurements of black holes over the next decade.
