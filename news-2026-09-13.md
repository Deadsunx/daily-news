## 1. BRICS Leaders Gather in New Delhi as Xi, Putin Meet Modi Amid US Pressure
*World & Geopolitics · CNBC*

India hosted the 18th BRICS Leaders' Summit at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi on September 12-13, with Prime Minister Narendra Modi welcoming Russian President Vladimir Putin, Chinese President Xi Jinping, Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and other bloc leaders. The summit addressed trade, development, finance, sustainability and technology cooperation, alongside discussion of the Russia-Ukraine war and the West Asia conflict. The gathering takes place amid pressure from the Trump administration on BRICS members over trade and geopolitical alignment.

## 2. Houthis Seize Red Sea Island as Saudi Arabia Shuts Key Oil Pipeline
*World & Geopolitics · NPR*

Houthi forces captured Yemen's Perim Island at the mouth of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait on September 11, according to Yemeni government sources, after also seizing the port city of Mocha and advancing on the coastal town of Dhubab. The same day, Saudi Arabia shut down its 745-mile East-West oil pipeline following a drone attack that Riyadh and Baghdad said originated from Iran-backed militias in Iraq. The pipeline had allowed Saudi Arabia to bypass congestion in the Strait of Hormuz caused by the ongoing US-Iran conflict. The combined disruptions pushed global oil prices back above $100 per barrel.

## 3. EU Approves $7.1 Billion in Air Defense Funding for Ukraine
*World & Geopolitics · Euromaidan Press*

The European Commission approved a €6.1 billion ($7.1 billion) tranche of funding for Ukraine on September 11, drawn from the EU's broader €90 billion ($104 billion) support loan. The money is earmarked for air and missile defense systems, ammunition, drones and electronic warfare equipment. The package includes derogations permitting Ukraine to purchase US-made PAC-3 interceptor missiles for Patriot batteries, including through NATO's PURL mechanism. PAC-3 interceptors are among the few weapons capable of shooting down Russian ballistic missiles.

## 6. Feather Preserved in Dinosaur Dropping Offers Clues to Bird Extinction Survival
*Science · ABC News (Australia)*

Researchers led by the University of Washington described the best-preserved fossil feather from the Mesozoic era, found embedded in a 66-million-year-old fossilized dinosaur dropping, or coprolite, from Montana's Hell Creek Formation. The specimen, discovered in 2016 by co-author David DeMar, is thought to be the remains of a bird eaten by a large theropod such as a Tyrannosaurus rex or Nanotyrannus. The findings, published in Current Biology, show the feather's structure was less effective at trapping air for insulation than those of many modern birds. The authors suggest this insulation difference may help explain why some ancient bird lineages died out in the mass extinction that killed roughly 75% of life on Earth, while others survived.

## 7. Chalmers Researchers Make Quantum Error-Correction Operations 1,000 Times Faster
*Science · ScienceDaily*

Researchers at Chalmers University of Technology in Sweden developed a method that performs certain quantum error-correction operations more than 1,000 times faster than previous approaches, compressing thousands of control cycles into a single driving cycle. The technique, described in Physical Review Letters by Tangyou Huang, Lei Du and Lingzhen Guo, applies to bosonic error-correcting codes including binomial, cat and Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill codes. Some operations were demonstrated completing in just a few microseconds. The approach could reduce error accumulation and bring fault-tolerant quantum computers closer to practical use.
