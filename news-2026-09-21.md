## 3. Federal Reserve Raises Interest Rates for First Time Since 2023
*Business & Markets · CNBC*

The Federal Open Market Committee voted unanimously on September 16 to increase the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00%, marking the first rate hike since 2023. The move comes as inflation remains elevated above the central bank's 2% target. Policymakers signaled one additional rate increase is expected before the end of 2026, with market expectations now pricing in three more hikes by mid-2027. The decision aims to support a timely return to the Fed's inflation target.

## 4. Consumer Sentiment Collapses to Second-Lowest Level on Record
*Business & Markets · CNBC*

The University of Michigan's consumer sentiment index plunged to 47.8 in early September, marking the second-lowest reading in data going back to 1952. The index fell 7.5 points month-over-month, well below expectations of 51.0, driven by inflation concerns and rising fuel prices. Inflation expectations surged to 4.6%, the highest level since June. Consumers expressed growing anxiety about household budgets and cited trade tensions as an additional economic headwind.

## 5. Twin Sisters Successfully Separated After Rare Cranial Surgery
*Science · CBS News*

Two-year-old twin sisters Hiyab and Wuhbto, born conjoined at the head, reached a major milestone in September after being successfully separated in a 24+ hour surgery at SSM Health Cardinal Glennon Children's Hospital in St. Louis. The surgery was performed in February 2026 by a team of 30 medical specialists who spent nearly a year planning the complex operation. Craniopagus (joined at the head) occurs in fewer than 6% of conjoined twin cases, with only 62 attempted separations worldwide. The twins are now seeing each other face-to-face for the first time since separation.

## 6. NASA's Roman Space Telescope Fuel Extended to Support 22-Year Mission
*Science · NASA*

NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has enough fuel to support at least 22 years of science operations, more than double its original 10-year design life, according to the space agency. The dramatic extension resulted from a highly accurate first trajectory correction on August 31 that used only 18 kilograms of the 200 kilograms allocated, achieving over 99% accuracy. Additional savings came from the telescope weighing less than expected at launch (8,056 kg vs. budgeted 9,800 kg), allowing fuel tanks to be filled to capacity. The extended fuel supply will significantly enhance the mission's scientific return.
