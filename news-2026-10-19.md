## 1. Brent crude settles at $109.86, highest close since May
*Business & Markets · Bloomberg via Rigzone*

Brent crude rose 3.7% to settle at $109.86 a barrel on Friday, its first close at that level since May, while WTI rose 3.5% to $101.41. Bloomberg attributed the gain to Houthi missile and drone strikes on Saudi Arabia, attacks on energy infrastructure in the Russia-Ukraine war, and fading prospects of near-term supply relief. Crude is up more than 75% this year and Brent posted a second straight weekly gain. The US has offered up to 17 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve as the White House faces pressure to lower energy costs before the November 3 midterms.

## 3. Hundreds of thousands protest across France over living costs and public services
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

Authorities said about 550,000 people protested nationwide on Saturday, including 110,000 in Paris, and the Interior Ministry reported at least 400 arrests. Tear gas was fired in Paris, and there were reports of looting in Marseille and a tourism office set ablaze in Lyon; the ministry blamed the unrest on the "ultra-left." The movement began in mid-September at a high school in Creteil and has since drawn students, teachers, nurses, farmers, unions including the CGT, and former "yellow vests." Protesters cite failing public services and rising living costs, while France faces high public debt and its highest borrowing costs in more than 20 years.

## 4. Former DRC president Joseph Kabila leads new rebel coalition
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

Former Democratic Republic of Congo President Joseph Kabila is leading the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation (FRN), which replaces the AFC-M23 alliance and accuses President Felix Tshisekedi of tyranny. The group says it will assume the AFC's obligations from the Doha negotiations, which cover a ceasefire, refugee returns and detainee releases. A Congolese military court sentenced Kabila to death in absentia last year on treason and war crimes charges, and government spokesman Patrick Muyaya said he has "nothing to offer but chaos." The development comes as the US pursues a DRC-Rwanda agreement and as conflict in the east has displaced millions.

## 5. Altman says OpenAI will build a humanoid robot, with demo expected in 2027
*AI & Tech · Engadget*

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said on the Sources podcast that the company will build a humanoid robot, saying the robot's "brain" matters more than its looks. He told Fortune he expects "a cool demo in 2027" and does not expect robots walking city streets for a few more years. OpenAI's careers page lists 27 robotics openings, including motion control, actuator control and ML infrastructure for physical AI. No product, price or release date has been announced.

## 6. Anthropic signs lease for first Canadian data center in Nova Scotia
*AI & Tech · Reuters via The Star (Malaysia)*

Anthropic signed a lease with EverWind for data center capacity at Point Tupper, Nova Scotia, its first data center agreement in Canada, according to Reuters. EverWind plans to invest more than C$10 billion in the campus and associated wind power, described as the largest privately funded investment in the province's history. Anthropic spent $7.33 billion on computing and infrastructure last year, roughly triple its 2024 spending. Data centers continue to face criticism over power and water use.

## 7. NASA's Roman Space Telescope image quality about 30% better than required
*Science · ScienceDaily (NASA)*

NASA reported that the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, launched August 30, 2026, is delivering image quality nearly 30% better than predicted. Measured optical error is 63 nanometers RMS against a pre-launch requirement of about 90 nanometers, after engineers moved the secondary mirror by about one-sixteenth the width of a human hair. The Wide Field Instrument, activated in September, has 18 detectors, and coronagraph light-blocking tests are planned in the coming weeks. The mission will study dark matter, dark energy and other cosmic questions.
