## 1. Trump says he will decide on resuming military action against Iran
*World & Geopolitics · Just Security (citing Axios and Reuters)*

President Trump said after meeting national security advisers that he will make a decision on resuming military action in the Iran war now that the U.S. midterm elections are over. Iran reportedly sent a nuclear proposal to Washington via Qatar, per Reuters. A senior Iranian official said it would be discussed only after the U.S. meets Iran's seven demands to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The outcome bears on regional security and on shipping through the strait.

## 3. METR estimates Anthropic's AI progress is running at about twice its prior pace
*AI & Tech · METR*

The independent evaluator METR published an assessment on Nov. 5 based on a high-access evaluation at Anthropic from Aug. 12 to Oct. 29, 2026. Its best estimate is that Anthropic's capabilities on well-specified tasks are improving at about 2x its March 2024 to October 2025 rate, with an uncertainty range that includes 1.3x to 3x. METR attributes roughly 80% of the acceleration to AI and says compute scaling did not cause it. Anthropic redacted most of the evidence METR found most informative, so the findings rely heavily on METR's judgment.

[METR report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/)

## 4. NASA's Swift observatory re-enters Earth's atmosphere after nearly 22 years
*Science · ScienceDaily (NASA material)*

NASA's Swift Observatory re-entered the atmosphere above North Sumatra, Indonesia, at 7:39 a.m. EST, according to the U.S. Space Force. Launched in 2004 with a planned two-year mission, it operated for nearly 22 years. Swift studied gamma-ray bursts and rapidly turned its three telescopes toward sudden events to alert observatories worldwide. NASA expected most of the spacecraft to burn up, though some components might have survived.

[ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261105224545.htm)

## 5. Paraguay's president resigns after major bank collapse
*World & Geopolitics · Just Security (citing The New York Times)*

According to a New York Times report summarized by Just Security, Paraguayan President Santiago Peña resigned after a major bank with close ties to his administration collapsed.
