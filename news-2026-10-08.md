## 1. At least 24 killed as Russia launches massive attack on Ukraine
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

Ukraine says Russia launched an overnight barrage of 130 drones, 48 cruise missiles and ballistic missiles across several regions. Ukrainian air defences intercepted 161 targets, according to Kyiv. ## 2. Brazil heads to presidential run-off between Flavio Bolsonaro and Lula
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

Flavio Bolsonaro, a far-right senator and son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, finished first in Brazil's presidential vote with 47 percent. Incumbent Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva was second with 45 percent. Neither passed the 50 percent threshold, so the two will face each other in a run-off on October 25, 2026. Thousands of students and other protesters marched against Bolsonaro in Rio de Janeiro on October 7.

## 3. Saudi Arabia says Houthi attacks on airports killed three foreign nationals
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority says three civilians were killed and 36 injured in Houthi-claimed attacks on two airports. At Abha International Airport, a Moroccan and an Algerian woman were killed and 28 people injured; at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, a Sudanese citizen was killed and eight injured. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a ballistic missile north of Riyadh and destroyed a launch platform in Sanaa. The US embassy in Riyadh issued another security alert.

## 5. Google's new SynthID website can identify AI-generated media
*AI & Tech · TechCrunch*

Google launched a public website at synthid.com that lets anyone check whether an image, video or audio clip was generated with AI. The tool was previously limited to selected journalists, media professionals and researchers. It detects SynthID watermarks, which Google applies to output from tools such as Gemini, Veo and Lyria; OpenAI, NVIDIA and Kakao also support SynthID. Google says people make about 1 million verification requests per day, and the article notes watermark detection tools are not infallible.

## 6. Microsoft releases new Nvidia-chip AI PCs with revamped Windows 11
*AI & Tech · TechCrunch*

Microsoft announced the Surface Laptop Ultra on October 7 at a San Francisco event, an AI-focused PC built on Nvidia's RTX Spark chip for running AI models locally. One base model starts at $2,600 and a model with a more powerful chip at $3,700, rising to as much as $5,900 with memory and storage upgrades. Microsoft also announced a Surface RTX Spark Dev Box starting at $6,000, and Dell's XPS 16 Creator Edition is priced at $3,800. The highest-end Surface model was already out of stock, and no general release date was given.
