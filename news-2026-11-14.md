## 1. US envoys meet Putin in Moscow, then Zelenskyy in Dublin, as Europe is set to join next round of Ukraine talks
*World & Geopolitics · Associated Press (via WHDH)*

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Russian President Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin for about two hours on Thursday, then met Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin for more than an hour. Earlier in the week they brokered talks in Abu Dhabi between Russian and Ukrainian delegations, the first direct discussions between the two sides in nine months, which produced no major breakthrough beyond an agreement on a large prisoner swap whose size has not been announced. Witkoff and Zelenskyy said Europe will take part in the next round, while Putin said European troops in Ukraine after a ceasefire would be "absolutely unacceptable."

## 2. UN climate chief urges speed as COP31 enters final week split over fossil fuels
*World & Geopolitics · Reuters (via Arab News)*

UN climate head Simon Stiell urged negotiators at COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye, to move quickly to the hardest issues and "avoid tactical delays" as the talks entered their second and final week. COP31 President of Negotiations Chris Bowen of Australia expects a first draft of the deal text early next week. Major fossil fuel producers and consumers are resisting firm new targets or a shift away from oil, gas and coal, while host Türkiye wants expanded global electrification at the centre of a deal. The United States is skipping the talks for a second year, and a UN report last month found the world on course to exceed 1.5°C of warming.

## 3. Senate Banking Committee asks ex-Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick to testify on chip exports
*AI & Tech · Fortune*

Senate Banking Committee chair Tim Scott asked former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify next month, citing national security concerns and the committee's oversight of advanced semiconductor export controls. The committee said it would use subpoenas if either declines to appear voluntarily. Sen. Jim Banks alleges Lutnick allowed high-end chips to reach China despite a presidential directive, and Sen. Elizabeth Warren says export-control loopholes remain open. Huang resigned from Nvidia this week amid personal-conduct reports that have drawn an FBI investigation, according to President Trump, and Nvidia's board has opened its own inquiry.

## 6. Preliminary study finds blood marker that may help detect advanced CTE in living people
*Science · Associated Press (via Daily Sitka Sentinel)*

Researchers at Washington University in St. Louis reported in JAMA that levels of a tau protein fragment, eMTBR-tau243, in blood were higher in people with advanced chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) than in healthy people. CTE, linked to repeated blows to the head, can currently be confirmed only after death. The study tested samples from 11 people classified as having CTE, and the marker could not reliably identify early-stage disease, so the findings are preliminary.

## 7. Tanker strikes river cruise boat on Mississippi in New Orleans; all aboard accounted for, 32 hospitalized
*World & Geopolitics · ABC News*

The tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck the American Cruise Lines paddleboat American Heritage near the Port of New Orleans at about 12:30 p.m. Friday, leaving the cruise boat significantly damaged and partly capsized. Nearly 200 people were aboard and all have been accounted for; 30 passengers and two officers were hospitalized with non-critical injuries. The Coast Guard said the tanker appears to have had a loss of steering, the NTSB is sending investigators, and the river is closed between mile markers 93 and 96.
