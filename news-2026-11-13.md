## 1. Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO; CFO Colette Kress named interim chief
*AI & Tech · Nvidia (SEC Form 8-K)*

Nvidia said Jensen Huang resigned as president, chief executive and board member effective immediately, according to a company release filed with the SEC on November 12. CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while remaining CFO, and former Synopsys CEO Harvey C. Jones was appointed the company's first-ever board chair. The board said it will consider internal and external candidates for a permanent CEO, and Nvidia will still report third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on November 17 as scheduled. The departure follows weeks of scrutiny over Huang's personal conduct and a related security investigation, as reported by multiple outlets.

## 4. NIH study links two common PFAS chemicals to higher rates of aggressive breast cancer
*Science · ScienceDaily (NIH / Journal of Clinical Oncology)*

An NIH-led analysis of the Sister Study, published in the Journal of Clinical Oncology, compared blood samples taken years before diagnosis from 1,609 women who developed breast cancer and 2,269 who did not. Women with the highest PFOA levels had a 67% higher rate of estrogen receptor-negative breast cancer, and those with the highest PFOS levels a 93% higher rate, compared with the lowest-exposure groups. Neither chemical showed a clear link to breast cancer overall, and the findings show an association, not causation. PFOA and PFOS have largely been phased out, but most people still carry measurable amounts, and newer replacement PFAS were not evaluated.

[Common Forever Chemicals Linked to Nearly Double the Rate of Aggressive Breast Cancer](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261112025527.htm)

## 5. A 1960 'Doomsday' paper set humanity's end for Friday, 13 November 2026
*Culture · Euronews*

In 1960, physicist Heinz von Foerster and colleagues published a paper in Science titled "Doomsday: Friday, 13 November, A.D. 2026," extrapolating about two millennia of population data to a curve that reached infinity on that date. The curve tracked real figures well until the early 1990s, after which population growth slowed sharply. Global growth peaked in 1963 at over 2% a year and fell below 1% by 2020, and UN estimates now put the population peak in the mid-2080s at about 10.3 billion. The date was chosen to draw attention to overpopulation, a heavily debated issue at the time.
