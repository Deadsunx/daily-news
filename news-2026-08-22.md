## 1. Trump Administration Announces New Phase of 'Economic Warfare' Against Iran
*World & Geopolitics · CBS News*

Vice President JD Vance said Thursday the war with Iran has entered a "new phase" centered on economic pressure, days after President Trump vowed an "economic D-Day" isolating Tehran. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said he will detail the sanctions Monday, calling the plan "the greatest coordinated economic isolation in the history of the world" and pressing allies including China to join. Iran's foreign minister dismissed the threat as a diversion from U.S. debt problems that would only increase hostility toward Washington. The announcement follows a U.S. naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz that had redirected 67 ships as of Thursday.

## 2. Israeli Settler Gunfire Kills Palestinian Teen Near Hebron
*World & Geopolitics · Associated Press*

An Israeli man guarding a group of settlers who had walked onto village land in Sa'ir, north of Hebron in the occupied West Bank, opened fire on Palestinians who confronted them Friday, killing 17-year-old Karim Sanad Raja Shalaldeh and wounding a 70-year-old man. The Israeli military described the settlers' entry as an "unauthorized hike," a practice Palestinians say is used to intimidate nearby villages. The killing came the same week the UN reported settler attacks on Palestinians in the West Bank have reached record levels in 2026, averaging more than six per day. Separately, six European countries and Canada condemned a new Israeli tender for over 1,200 settlement homes east of Jerusalem.

## 3. Walmart Beats Revenue Estimates, Raises Outlook Despite Tariff Costs
*Business & Markets · WWD*

Walmart reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $187.9 billion, up 5.9%, topping Wall Street estimates, while global e-commerce sales grew 23% and its advertising business grew 38%. The company missed earnings expectations for the first time since May 2022, and U.S. comparable sales grew just 2.6%, its slowest pace in six years. Walmart raised its full-year sales growth outlook to 3.75%-4.75% and adjusted EPS guidance to $2.52-$2.62, helped in part by a $2.9 billion tariff refund following a Supreme Court ruling against Trump-era tariffs. Walmart shares fell despite the beat, with the earnings miss weighing on the broader stock market Thursday.

## 5. Nvidia Strikes $6 Billion Licensing Deal With AI Coding Startup Poolside
*AI & Tech · Newcomer*

Nvidia agreed to a non-exclusive $6 billion licensing deal for access to Poolside's "Model Factory" AI model-building platform, and separately invested $1 billion in the startup at a $12 billion pre-money valuation. As part of the arrangement, 109 Poolside employees have received job offers to join Nvidia, though the startup's founders are remaining to run the company independently. Poolside described the deal as neither an acquisition nor an acquihire. The transaction gives Nvidia access to Poolside's model-building technology and talent without a formal takeover.

## 6. Anthropic Targets IPO That Could Rival or Exceed SpaceX's Record
*AI & Tech · Bloomberg*

Anthropic is preparing an initial public offering it expects to match or exceed the roughly $86.2 billion raised in SpaceX's record IPO, and could confidentially file paperwork with the SEC as soon as the end of August, according to Bloomberg. The AI company is working with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase on the offering. The move follows a $65 billion financing round in May that valued Anthropic at $965 billion. Anthropic reported positive adjusted operating income in the second quarter despite a large net loss in 2025.
