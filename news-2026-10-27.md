## 1. Lula wins Brazil presidential runoff; Bolsonaro refuses to accept result
*World & Geopolitics · ABC News (Australia)*

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won Brazil's presidential runoff with 51.8% of the vote to 48.2% for Flávio Bolsonaro, a lead of nearly 4.4 million votes, securing a fourth term. Bolsonaro said it was impossible to acknowledge the result, criticised the Supreme Court's extension of voting hours, and called for the election to be annulled. Legal experts quoted by ABC News (Australia) say a challenge has little legal basis. The outcome ends a tight race that polls had shown as close.

## 2. EQT to acquire Range Resources in all-stock deal
*Business & Markets · EQT Corporation (SEC filing)*

EQT Corporation announced on October 26 that it will acquire Range Resources in a 100% stock transaction, with each Range share converting into 0.987 EQT shares. The combined company would have a pro forma enterprise value above $50 billion, with EQT holders owning about 73% and Range holders about 27%. EQT expects about $325 million in annual base synergies within two years, and the deal is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory and shareholder approvals. Two Range representatives, including CEO Dennis Degner, would join EQT's board.

## 4. Phase III trial: silver diamine fluoride liquid halts cavities in young children
*Science · ScienceDaily (JAMA Pediatrics, University of Michigan)*

A Phase III trial led by the University of Michigan tested 38% silver diamine fluoride, a liquid painted onto teeth without drilling or injections, in 830 children under age 6 in Michigan, New York and Iowa. Applied at six-month intervals, it stopped decay in more than half of the affected baby teeth. The results were published in JAMA Pediatrics. The treatment permanently darkens decayed tooth areas, and dentists currently use it off-label for cavities; the data is intended to support an FDA application.

[ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261025222339.htm)

## 5. JWST finds three actively feeding black holes in galaxy from early universe
*Science · ScienceDaily (Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics)*

Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope identified three actively feeding supermassive black holes in galaxy J0148-4214, seen as it was about 1.2 billion years after the Big Bang. Two lie near the galaxy's center about 620 light-years apart and are expected to merge within a few hundred million years. The third lies roughly 5,500 light-years out, and one of the three appears to be feeding faster than the Eddington limit. The study, led by the Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics, was published in Astronomy & Astrophysics.
