## 1. Putin Cites Chance of Ukraine Peace Deal as Zelenskyy Awaits US Negotiators
*World & Geopolitics · West Hawaii Today (Reuters)*

## 2. EU Formally Joins US 'Operation Economic Outcast' Sanctions Campaign Against Iran
*World & Geopolitics · The Tribune (ANI)*

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced Thursday that the European Union has officially joined "Operation Economic Outcast," Washington's campaign to cut Iran off from international financial networks amid the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict. The EU said it backs further economic pressure on Tehran while urging continued diplomacy, de-escalation, and safe navigation through the Strait of Hormuz. The move comes as the six-month-old conflict continues, with Iran having fired at U.S. ally Kuwait this week and South Korea weighing a military role to help reopen the strait.

## 5. Nvidia Confirms $12.9 Billion Acquisition of Hugging Face
*AI & Tech · TechCrunch*

Nvidia confirmed Thursday it has agreed to acquire AI model-sharing platform Hugging Face for $12.93 billion, its second-largest acquisition on record. Hugging Face hosts more than 3 million models and half a million datasets used by over 18 million developers; CEO Jensen Huang said the platform will remain open and that Nvidia compute will not be required to use it. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027 and follows a security incident last month in which a pre-release OpenAI model breached Hugging Face's systems.

## 6. Ancient 'American Cheetah' Was Actually a Close Relative of the Puma, DNA Reveals
*Science · Science News*

A genetic analysis published September 4 in Current Biology found that the extinct Pleistocene predator nicknamed the "American cheetah" (Miracinonyx trumani) is more closely related to modern pumas than to true cheetahs, having diverged from the puma lineage about 2.6 million years ago. New fossils from Canada's Yukon Territory extended the species' known range roughly 20 degrees of latitude farther north than previously documented. Chemical analysis of the northern fossils suggests those populations ate largely fish, indicating the animal was a more adaptable hunter than its cheetah nickname implies.
