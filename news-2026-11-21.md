## 2. UN top court finds Myanmar responsible for genocide against Rohingya
*World & Geopolitics · UN News*

The International Court of Justice ruled in The Gambia v. Myanmar that Myanmar is responsible for genocide against the Rohingya and failed to prevent and punish it, the first such ruling against a state under the Genocide Convention, UN News reported. The case, brought by The Gambia in November 2019, concerns 2017 clearance operations in northern Rakhine state after which some 700,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh and an estimated 10,000 were killed. Myanmar must report to the court within two years on restitution and compensation, and the judgment is final with no appeal.

[Read at UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/11/1168836)

## 3. UK and EU agree youth mobility scheme at Brussels summit, defer tuition fee decision
*World & Geopolitics · The PIE News*

The UK and EU agreed a Youth Experience Scheme at their summit in Brussels on November 20, allowing 100,000 young people from each side to live, work, study or travel in the other's territory for up to three years, The PIE News reported. Both sides aim to apply the agreement by 2028 and make opportunities available no later than January 2030, with UK participation in Erasmus+ starting in 2027. A decision on tuition fees for students crossing borders was pushed back, with the two sides committing to a supplementary agreement within six months.

## 6. Study dates 'Taung Child' fossil to about 3 million years and points to more hominin species
*Science · ScienceDaily*

A study led by Professor Gabriele Macho of Vrije Universiteit Brussel dates the Taung Child fossil, found in South Africa in 1924, to between 2.96 and 3.03 million years ago. Based on tooth structure and patterns of brain and dental development, the researchers argue that several adult fossils classed as Australopithecus africanus may represent distinct hominin species from the Sterkfontein region. They point to repeated shifts between wetter and drier climates in southern Africa as a possible driver of that diversity.
