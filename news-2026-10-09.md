## 2. WHO seeks answers after lab worker's death at Russian plague institute
*World & Geopolitics · Democracy Now!*

A worker at the Irkutsk Anti-Plague Research Institute in southern Siberia died on October 2, and Russian officials ordered about 200 people to quarantine. Russia's public health agency denies reports of a second pneumonic plague case, saying thousands of tests found no evidence of transmission. WHO chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said many questions remain unanswered and that WHO has requested more information on the lab tests and contacts. The U.S. issued a formal diplomatic complaint on Wednesday, and President Trump said he does not believe the death was related to a bioweapon.

## 5. Webb telescope finds signs of violent planetary collisions around young stars
*Science · ScienceDaily (NASA)*

NASA's James Webb Space Telescope studied 21 rare 'extreme debris disks' around young stars, a stage seen in only about 1% of young stars. About one-third of the disks are silica-rich, likely from very energetic collisions between Mars-sized bodies around stars younger than 300 million years; about two-thirds are silica-poor, likely from lower-energy impacts. The results, published October 1 in The Astrophysical Journal, may offer a view of collisions like the one that likely formed the Moon.
