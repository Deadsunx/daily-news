## 1. US and Iran trade opposing claims over control of the Strait of Hormuz
*World & Geopolitics · CBS News*

Tehran said Thursday that the Strait of Hormuz is "under Iran's control and management," directly contradicting President Donald Trump, who said the United States has "total control" of the waterway and wrote on Truth Social, "I THINK WE WILL KEEP IT." Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Washington had miscalculated in the war because of intelligence failures and risked a "bigger miscalculation" over the strait. Iran has also demanded compensation for an oil spill in the strait that washed up on Qeshm Island, saying a foreign bulk carrier appeared to be the source. The dispute keeps the world's most important oil chokepoint effectively closed nearly six months into the conflict.

## 2. Ukrainian strike on Russian Black Sea port halts two major grain terminals
*World & Geopolitics · Reuters*

A Ukrainian attack on the Russian Black Sea port city of Novorossiysk hit warships at a naval base and forced two major grain terminals to halt operations, according to Russian and Ukrainian officials. Regional Governor Veniamin Kondratyev said the strikes killed at least two people, including an 8-year-old child. Because Russia is the world's largest wheat exporter, the damage to the grain terminals raised concerns about disruption to global supplies. Chicago wheat futures rose about 3% following the attack.

## 4. IEA sees global oil demand contracting in 2026 as crude prices slide
*Business & Markets · TheStreet*

The International Energy Agency said global oil demand is expected to contract by 1.6 million barrels per day in 2026, citing high fuel prices, product shortages tied to maritime disruptions, and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Crude prices fell on the forecast and on easing demand expectations, with West Texas Intermediate down 1.84% to $81.74 a barrel and Brent down 1.80% to $87.38. A contraction of that size would be an unusual reversal for global consumption, which has grown in almost every non-recession year. Falling crude also helped lift equities by easing near-term inflation pressure.

## 5. Taiwan says government agencies were targeted by AI-assisted hacking campaign
*AI & Tech · Reuters*

Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs said it detected AI-assisted cyberattacks on government agencies last month and that the affected bodies successfully handled the incident. Investigators found the attacks displayed clear characteristics of an "overseas source." The ministry said the hackers used a hybrid approach combining manual operations with AI agent-assisted attacks. The disclosure is one of the more explicit government statements to date that autonomous AI agents are being used operationally in state-linked intrusion campaigns.

## 6. Hassabis pitched an independent AI oversight body before leaving DeepMind
*AI & Tech · The Wall Street Journal*

## 7. Total solar eclipse crosses Greenland, Iceland and Spain
*Science · CNN*

A total solar eclipse swept across Greenland, Iceland, the North Atlantic and Spain, with a small corner of Portugal also in the path of totality. Maximum duration of totality was 2 minutes and 18 seconds. It was the first time since 1905 that mainland Spain fell under complete totality, and Spanish authorities deployed more than 33,500 law enforcement officers to observation areas and access points. Iceland expected up to 20,000 additional overseas visitors during an already busy peak tourist season.
