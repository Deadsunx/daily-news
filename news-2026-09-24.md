## 1. Russian Strikes on Kyiv and Donetsk Kill Six Hours After Trump-Zelenskyy Talks
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

Russian drone strikes killed six people in Ukraine on September 23 — two in Kyiv and four in the front-line Donetsk region — with more than 30 others injured, hours after U.S. President Donald Trump and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy discussed a potential energy-related ceasefire on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly. The rare daytime attack on Kyiv lasted from morning into early afternoon using fast, jet-powered drones, striking a nine-story building, a business center, warehouses, a petrol station and railway infrastructure, according to Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko and Ukrainian officials. Zelenskyy said he was ready for "any format" of an energy ceasefire if Russia stopped attacking Ukrainian infrastructure, though no agreements were reached in the Trump meeting. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said there were "no grounds" yet for peace talks, while noting President Vladimir Putin remains open to meeting Trump one-on-one.

*World & Geopolitics · The Christian Science Monitor*

Trade, artificial intelligence governance, Taiwan and China's support for Iran are expected to top the agenda, with both sides possibly extending the one-year trade truce reached in Busan, South Korea, last October, under which China paused some rare-earth export controls and agreed to buy U.S. soybeans while Washington lowered some tariffs. ## 4. Qualcomm Unveils Dual 2nm Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 Chips With Bigger On-Device AI
*AI & Tech · Engadget*

Qualcomm introduced two new flagship mobile processors, the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 and the higher-end Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6, both built on a 2-nanometer process with an eight-core Oryon CPU featuring prime cores clocked up to 5GHz. ## 5. Large Swedish Study Links Rare Pregnancy Infections to Tripled Autism Risk
*Science · Karolinska Institutet (via ScienceDaily)*

A Karolinska Institutet study of 3.7 million people born in Sweden between 1987 and 2021 found that children with a congenital TORCH infection — passed from a pregnant woman to the fetus — were about three times more likely to be diagnosed with autism and more than seven times more likely to have an intellectual disability. Among the 975 people identified with a congenital TORCH infection, the risk of severe to profound intellectual disability was up to 30 times higher than in unaffected children, according to the study published in JAMA Pediatrics. Researchers estimate these infections, which include cytomegalovirus, rubella, toxoplasma and herpesvirus, account for about 0.034% of autism cases and 1.2% of severe intellectual disability cases in Sweden, and sibling comparisons suggested the association was not explained by shared family factors. Lead author Hugo Sjöqvist called it the largest study to date on the topic, and researchers noted that some of these infections, such as rubella, can be prevented through vaccination.
