## 1. Trump and Xi Meet at White House, Extend Trade Truce, Discuss AI and Iran
*World & Geopolitics · NPR*

President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping held a two-day state visit at the White House this week that included a formal dinner attended by tech executives such as Elon Musk, Tim Cook, Jeff Bezos, Sam Altman and Jensen Huang. The two sides announced an extension of the trade truce first reached in South Korea last year and discussed a system for warning each other of AI incidents that could pose national security risks, though Trump said he wants AI regulation left "exactly where it is." Iran was also on the agenda, as the U.S. continues a naval blockade on Iranian ports in the Strait of Hormuz that has cut off an estimated 12% of China's oil imports, with analysts saying Beijing could help broker a resolution. Xi also announced that two Chinese pandas will be sent to Zoo Atlanta.

## 2. Oil Prices Slip as US and Iran Explore Phased Reopening of Strait of Hormuz
*Business & Markets · Reuters (via The Sunday Guardian)*

Brent crude fell $1.47, or 1.4%, to $105.11 a barrel on Friday, while U.S. benchmark WTI dropped $2, or 2.1%, to $92.61, as markets weighed reports that U.S. and Iranian negotiators in New York are exploring a phased plan in which Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for the U.S. lifting its economic blockade, according to Reuters. The pullback followed a volatile Thursday session in which both benchmarks jumped as much as 5% intraday after renewed Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia raised fresh supply concerns. Reuters estimated that roughly one-fifth of the world's oil and gas shipments have been curtailed since the Iran war began at the end of February. Despite Friday's decline, Brent remained up about 2.1% for the week.

## 3. Google to Launch Prototype AI Data Center Satellite on SpaceX Rocket
*AI & Tech · Space.com*

Google announced that a prototype satellite for its Project Suncatcher AI-computing constellation will fly aboard SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare mission, currently scheduled to launch October 1. The satellite, built by Earth-imaging company Planet, carries four Google tensor processing units (TPUs) providing roughly the computing power of one data-center server, and will operate in orbit for about a year while Google studies how space radiation and heat affect the chips. Google plans to launch two more Suncatcher satellites in 2027 to test laser communication between spacecraft, working toward an eventual cluster of dozens of satellites; a single Falcon 9 launch costs about $74 million. Google research lead James Manyika said the company does not expect a fully operational AI data center in space for several years.

## 4. Astronomers Discover the Milky Way's First Black Hole-Powered 'Microblazar'
*Science · Space.com*

Astronomers have identified the Milky Way's first known microblazar, a stellar-mass black hole system named IRAS 18293-0941 located about 12,000 light-years away that fires near-light-speed jets almost directly at Earth. The black hole, with a mass up to a few hundred times that of the sun, feeds on a massive companion star it orbits every 11.4 days; observations from the European VLBI Network and the MeerKAT radio telescope confirmed the jet's origin and revealed a 100-light-year-wide bubble where it strikes interstellar gas, producing gamma-ray photons roughly ten times more energetic than particles in the Large Hadron Collider. The system had gone undetected for decades because it sits behind a thick screen of interstellar dust, said researchers led by Josep Marti of the University of Jaen. The findings are posted on the preprint server arXiv and have been accepted for publication in the journal Astronomy & Astrophysics.

## 5. World No. 1 Jannik Sinner Withdraws from China Open as Knee Injury Lingers
*Sports · Al Jazeera (Reuters)*

World number one Jannik Sinner has withdrawn from the China Open, saying he is not yet ready to compete as he continues recovering from the knee injury that also forced him to skip the US Open. The 25-year-old Italian, who has not played since winning his fifth major title at Wimbledon in July, had already pulled out of the Canadian Open and Cincinnati Open this summer. His withdrawal leaves the tournament, which runs September 30 to October 6, without its defending champion, with 24-time Grand Slam winner Novak Djokovic, now ranked world number 12 after a first-round exit at the US Open, among the headline attractions in Beijing.
