## 1. Indonesia Battles Widening Wildfire Haze as Residents Pray for Rain
*World & Geopolitics · NPR (via AP)*

Huge peatland and forest fires across Indonesia's Sumatra and Kalimantan islands have blanketed cities in choking haze, with South Sumatra recording the most hot spots nationwide (1,429), followed by West Kalimantan (1,226) and Central Kalimantan (811). About 3,000 residents gathered in Palembang on Tuesday to perform a special Islamic rain prayer as authorities deployed 30 aircraft for cloud-seeding over Kalimantan and 22 helicopters over Sumatra. Indonesian police have arrested 72 people suspected of starting the fires, and Forestry Ministry data show nearly 940 square kilometers burned in July alone. The smoke has drifted across borders into Malaysia and Brunei.

## 2. US Treasury Unveils 'Operation Economic Outcast' Sanctions Targeting Iran
*World & Geopolitics · NPR*

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced a new round of sanctions Monday aimed at severing Iran's financial lifelines, targeting a network of brokers, companies and shadow-fleet vessels operating across the UAE, Hong Kong, China, Singapore and Switzerland that channel oil revenue to the IRGC-Qods Force. The administration, calling the campaign "Operation Economic Outcast," expanded secondary-sanctions categories to digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping, and said it is giving trading partners time to cut ties with Tehran before further action. Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei vowed a "seismic" retaliation and warned oil tankers transiting the Persian Gulf could be targeted. Iran's rial has plunged past 2 million to the dollar amid inflation near 90%, according to Iran's Statistical Center.

## 3. UK to Share Classified Missile Technology to Help Ukraine Build Long-Range SCALP Weapons
*World & Geopolitics · NPR (via AP)*

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham, visiting Kyiv for Ukraine's Independence Day, said Monday that defense firm MBDA will release classified technical details on UK-made components of the SCALP missile so Ukraine can assemble the weapons domestically. The SCALP, a French-produced version of Britain's Storm Shadow, has a range of more than 250 kilometers and is designed to strike hardened bunkers and infrastructure. Experts cautioned the move won't yield operational missiles soon, with production unlikely before next year, and won't address Ukraine's more urgent need for defense against Russian ballistic missile strikes. The Kremlin accused the UK of "fanning the flame" of the war, a charge Burnham dismissed.

## 5. OpenAI Bans Russian ChatGPT Accounts Behind Covert 'Sovereignty Index' Influence Campaign
*AI & Tech · OpenAI*

OpenAI said Tuesday it banned a cluster of ChatGPT accounts operating from Russia via VPNs that generated social media content promoting the International Burke Institute, a fabricated "expert community" claiming a base in Israel. The accounts produced posts for X, LinkedIn, Facebook, Substack and Telegram promoting the institute's "sovereignty index," which praised Russia and criticized Western countries including France, Germany and the US; a review of 36 articles on the IBI website found 34 were copied or misattributed from real academic work. OpenAI assessed the operation's reach as limited, with most posts drawing low view counts and its Telegram channels attracting 10,000-20,000 followers each, but said the elaborate fake-institution infrastructure distinguished it from prior Russia-linked campaigns disrupted since the war in Ukraine began. OpenAI does not allow ChatGPT access from Russia, and the operators used VPNs to evade that restriction.

## 6. NASA Encapsulates Roman Space Telescope Ahead of Aug. 30 Launch
*Science · NASA*

NASA said Monday that its Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has been encapsulated inside the payload fairing of a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket at Kennedy Space Center, completing final launch preparations. The 43-foot-tall fairing will shield the observatory from acoustic vibration, aerodynamic pressure and heating during ascent before separating a few minutes into flight. NASA and SpaceX are targeting no earlier than 7:26 a.m. EDT on Sunday, Aug. 30, for liftoff, after which Roman will travel to the Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 2 to begin surveys of exoplanets, dark matter and dark energy. Teams completed encapsulation on Aug. 21 and photographed the enclosed spacecraft on Aug. 24 ahead of transport to the launch pad.
