## 1. Israeli Strike Near Gaza City Port Kills Six, IDF Says It Targeted Hamas Nukhba Commanders
*World & Geopolitics · The Times of Israel*

An Israeli airstrike hit a cafe near the port of Gaza City on Aug. 18, killing six people and wounding about ten others, according to Palestinian health officials. The Israeli military said the strike targeted a meeting of commanders and operatives from Hamas's elite Nukhba force, including a company commander and three platoon commanders, one of whom allegedly took part in the Oct. 7, 2023 attack on Israel. A senior Israeli minister said the same day that Israel would not halt attacks in Gaza, with strikes across the territory reported to have killed roughly 17 to 18 people in total. The strike underscores continued military activity even as a U.S.-brokered framework to disarm Hamas remains under negotiation.

## 2. Ukraine's Parliament Returns From Recess as Zelensky Weighs Permanent Defense Minister Pick
*World & Geopolitics · The Kyiv Independent*

Ukraine's Verkhovna Rada resumed session on Aug. 18 after a summer recess that began in mid-July following President Volodymyr Zelensky's dismissal of popular Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov, which triggered mass protests. Zelensky has not formally nominated permanent successors for the defense and foreign ministries; Yevhen Khmara, a former acting SBU chief, has served as acting defense minister since July, while Andrii Sybiha has continued as acting foreign minister. Protesters continue to gather near parliament calling for Fedorov's reinstatement, a move Zelensky has ruled out, though he has offered Fedorov another senior government role focused on defense technology, which Fedorov has rejected. Under Ukrainian law, Khmara, a major general, would need to leave military service before taking the post permanently.

## 3. Home Depot Tops Q2 Estimates as Sales Rise 5.7% to $47.9 Billion
*Business & Markets · CNBC*

Home Depot reported second-quarter fiscal 2026 sales of $47.9 billion, up 5.7% from a year earlier, with net earnings of $4.8 billion. Adjusted diluted earnings per share came in at $4.92, beating the $4.73 analyst estimate, while comparable sales rose 1.7% (U.S. comparable sales up 1.3%). The company reaffirmed its full-year guidance, still expecting comparable sales to range from flat to up 2% and total sales to grow roughly 2.5% to 4.5%. The results, released Aug. 18, come as investors watch how home-improvement retailers are faring amid a sluggish housing market.

## 4. Klarna Shares Plunge Nearly 20% After Guidance Cut, CFO and CMO Departures
*Business & Markets · Yahoo Finance*

Klarna shares fell about 19% on Aug. 18 after the Swedish buy-now-pay-later firm returned to quarterly profit but lowered its full-year outlook. The company now expects 2026 revenue of $4.08 billion to $4.16 billion, down from earlier guidance above $4.34 billion, and cut its gross merchandise volume forecast to $149 billion-$151 billion from more than $155 billion, citing currency effects and softer European volume concentrated in Germany. Klarna also announced that CFO Niclas Neglén and chief marketing officer David Sandström will both step down in early 2027, with Neglén having overseen the company's stock market debut last September.

## 5. Nvidia to Back $105 Billion in Financing for OpenAI's New Ohio Data Center
*AI & Tech · CNBC*

Nvidia will guarantee up to $105 billion in financing for a new AI data center in Pike County, Ohio, that OpenAI will lease for 20 years, according to a securities filing disclosed Aug. 17. The facility, to be built and managed by SB Energy at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus, will start with 4.25 gigawatts of computing capacity with an option to expand to 8 gigawatts, with capacity coming online in phases starting in 2028. SoftBank and SB Energy plan to build supporting power infrastructure for 10 gigawatts of energy and invest at least $4.2 billion in regional grid upgrades, while Nvidia separately will invest $1.5 billion directly in SB Energy. OpenAI said the project is expected to support 35,000 construction jobs through 2032 and 2,500 permanent positions.
