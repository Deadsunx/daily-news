## 1. Trump Tells UN He Will "Annihilate" Iran Absent a Nuclear Deal
*World & Geopolitics · UPI*

President Trump told the 81st UN General Assembly on September 22 that he faces "a big decision" on Iran: either a deal that lets it "rebuild and create a far greater country than it ever was before," or he will "annihilate the Islamic republic, and do it quickly." He called Iran "the No. 1 sponsor of terror" and pointed to the joint U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran that began February 28 as evidence he has "acted" while others talked. He also called Cuba "an absolutely failed state" and the International Criminal Court a "rogue institution," while addressing the U.S. capture of Venezuela's Nicolas Maduro. The speech came amid reports that Tehran is waiting to see how Republicans fare in November's midterm elections before agreeing to a deal.

## 2. Russia and Ukraine Trade Deadly Overnight Strikes as Zelenskyy Heads to Meet Trump
*World & Geopolitics · The Associated Press*

Russia struck at least four Ukrainian regions overnight with missiles and more than 200 drones, killing four people in Dnipro and wounding five in Kyiv, while Russia's defense ministry said it downed 297 Ukrainian drones over 14 regions and Crimea. Ukraine struck Rosneft's Kuibyshev oil refinery in Samara, Russia, sparking a large fire that killed one person and wounded four, according to regional governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev. The exchange came as Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy traveled to New York for the UN General Assembly to meet President Trump and seek additional Patriot air-defense systems more than four and a half years into the war. French President Emmanuel Macron separately discussed supplying Ukraine with radar systems and a possible Black Sea energy-strike moratorium.

## 4. Anthropic Releases Claude Opus 5.5 at Lower Prices, Claims Best-Ever Performance
*AI & Tech · TechCrunch*

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 on Tuesday, calling it "the strongest-performing model we've tested to date" and saying it outperforms the company's larger Fable model on many benchmarks, roughly two months after Opus 5 launched on July 24. Output pricing drops to $20 per million tokens from $25 for the prior model, and the new model also runs faster. Because Opus 5.5 matches Anthropic's Mythos model in biology and cybersecurity risk, it triggers the same safety restrictions used for Fable, with safety testing involving outside groups METR and Frontier Design. Anthropic said Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 are expected "in the coming weeks" with similar improvements.

## 5. OpenAI Launches Cheaper GPT-6 Sol and Luna Models 90 Minutes After Anthropic's Opus 5.5
*AI & Tech · TechCrunch*

OpenAI on Tuesday launched updated Sol and Luna models in its GPT-6 family, roughly 90 minutes after Anthropic released its own Opus 5.5 model. Sol is aimed at complex tasks such as coding, while Luna targets high-volume tasks like summarizing documents or answering quick questions; API pricing for both is half that of the prior 5.6-series models. OpenAI said GPT-6 Sol makes about half as many mistakes as its predecessor on the company's internal factuality evaluation, calling it "Astra-level reliability at much lower cost," and claimed both new models outperform Anthropic's Fable and Opus models. The models are available immediately in ChatGPT Work and Codex and via the API, with Luna also rolling out to desktop, Free, and Go users.

## 6. Waymo Expands Teen Rider Accounts to Nashville
*Business & Markets · TechCrunch*

Waymo began opening teen accounts for riders ages 13 to 17 in Nashville on Tuesday, its second city to do so after debuting the program in Phoenix last year. Teen accounts must be linked to a parent or guardian, and once activated, teens can hail rides anywhere in Waymo's Nashville service area and bring up to three guests in the same age range. A company spokesperson called Nashville the "first of many" additional teen launches planned in the coming months, contingent on regulatory approval in new markets. Waymo's company-wide paid trips have grown from 50,000 a week in May 2024 to more than half a million a week currently, with the company launching in a dozen cities over the past two years, including Denver, Las Vegas, San Diego, and Tampa in the past month alone.
