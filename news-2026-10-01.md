## 6. Alzheimer's risk gene APOE4 may have a reversible weakness in brain blood vessels
*Science · ScienceDaily (Mount Sinai press release)*

Mount Sinai researchers reported in Cell that, in the presence of APOE4, brain blood-vessel pericytes turn into scar-producing myofibroblast-like cells, promoting vascular fibrosis and more amyloid around vessels. Blocking TGF-β signaling restored pericyte coverage and reduced fibrosis and amyloid, a result reproduced in aged APOE4 mice. A companion Cell Stem Cell study using lab-grown 3D human brain tissue ('miBrains') found APOE4 caused cholesterol buildup in astrocytes, impairing clearance of alpha-synuclein. APOE4 is described as the strongest known genetic risk factor for Alzheimer's, which affects more than 7 million older adults in the US.

[ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260930225450.htm)

## 7. Record-breaking X-ray flash may reveal the birth of a magnetar
*Science · ScienceDaily (ERC BHianca)*

A study in Science Bulletin describes EP250704a/GRB 250704B, detected on July 4, 2025, in which a gamma-ray burst lasting about half a second was followed by bright X-ray emission recorded by the Einstein Probe for nearly ten minutes. The authors call it the longest-lasting prompt X-ray flash observed from a neutron star merger. Follow-up with the Very Large Telescope measured a redshift of z=0.6610, meaning the light traveled more than six billion years, and deep observations found no supernova, which the team treats as evidence for a neutron star merger. They suggest the remnant may be a magnetar sustaining the emission.
