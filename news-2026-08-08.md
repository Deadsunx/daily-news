## 1. Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkiye sign trilateral joint defence pact in Mecca
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkiye signed a joint defence agreement in Mecca on Friday, Pakistan's foreign ministry said, stating that an armed attack on any one of the three countries would be regarded as an attack against them all. The deal was signed by Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who had arrived in Saudi Arabia a day earlier accompanied by army chief Asim Munir. It extends to Turkiye a mutual-defence commitment comparable to the bilateral Saudi-Pakistan pact concluded in September 2025. The agreement follows repeated missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia by Iran and allied groups since the US and Israel attacked Iran on February 28.

## 2. Emerging Iran-Oman deal would recognise Iranian control over Strait of Hormuz traffic
*World & Geopolitics · Fortune*

A proposed agreement between Iran and Oman to reopen the Strait of Hormuz would give Iran control over inbound traffic, sources told Reuters, a departure from the prewar status of full freedom of navigation that the US and Gulf states have demanded. Unresolved points include how Iran's control is defined, its role over outbound traffic, and transit fees: Iran is seeking 5 to 7 percent of the value of cargoes, while Oman has floated about 3 percent. A US official told the Washington Post that any temporary routes must involve "no approvals or permissions and no tolls or charges," and Washington is not a party to the Oman-Iran talks. Months of US-Israeli strikes have not removed Iran's ability to threaten shipping, leaving the strait effectively closed and Gulf oil supplies bottled up.

## 3. US payrolls fell by 23,000 in July as unemployment held at 4.1 percent
*Business & Markets · U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics*

Total US nonfarm payroll employment declined by 23,000 in July, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, against a Dow Jones consensus forecast of an 83,000 gain. The unemployment rate was 4.1 percent with 6.9 million people unemployed, while the labor force participation rate fell to 61.4 percent, down 0.7 percentage point since January. Employment fell in local government education (-50,000), retail trade (-19,000) and financial activities (-14,000), while health care continued to trend up (+22,000). Payroll figures for May and June were revised down by a combined 103,000, and average hourly earnings rose 3.2 percent over the year to $37.62.

## 5. New Mexico judge orders Meta to pay $567 million over harms to young users
*AI & Tech · Associated Press*

Judge Bryan Biedscheid ordered Meta Platforms late Thursday to pay $567 million in the second phase of New Mexico's trial over alleged harms to young people on Facebook and Instagram. Of that sum, $420 million is directed to treatment services for young people, with the remainder funding awareness, prevention and screening efforts over the next five years. The order comes on top of $375 million in civil penalties imposed by jurors in March, bringing Meta's total liability in the case to about $942 million. Plaintiffs had also asked the court to require changes to addictive features, age verification and default privacy settings; Meta has said it plans to appeal.

## 6. Tesla and SpaceX commit $16.8 billion to a Texas semiconductor complex
*AI & Tech · Tech Startups*

Tesla and SpaceX are jointly backing a semiconductor manufacturing complex called Terafab in Grimes County, Texas, with an initial investment of $16.8 billion, according to reporting by the Wall Street Journal. The plant is intended to manufacture, package and test memory and logic chips for Tesla vehicles and Optimus robots as well as SpaceX computing systems, on a site that could eventually span roughly 100 million square feet. Both companies currently depend on external foundries and suppliers, so the project represents a move toward vertical integration of their chip supply. Large semiconductor plants require substantial electricity, water and specialised labour, and Texas officials say the project would create thousands of jobs.

## 7. WHO recommends Ervebo trial as DR Congo's Ebola cases pass 4,000
*Science · Al Jazeera*

The Democratic Republic of the Congo has recorded 4,053 confirmed Bundibugyo Ebola cases and 1,850 deaths since the outbreak was declared in mid-May, according to the latest health ministry situation report. The outbreak has reached 53 health zones across five provinces, with 87 percent of cases in Ituri and 11 percent in North Kivu, and is the second-largest Ebola epidemic on record after West Africa's 2014-2016 outbreak, which exceeded 28,000 cases. On Friday, WHO vaccine experts recommended that Ervebo, the only licensed Ebola vaccine, be prioritised for a randomised clinical trial to test cross-protection against Bundibugyo, for which no vaccine exists. In animal studies, three of four non-human primates vaccinated with Ervebo survived Bundibugyo challenge versus one of four controls; Gavi holds a stockpile of 500,000 Ervebo doses.
