## 1. Israel rejects Trump's 15-point Gaza plan, straining ties with Washington
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu told his cabinet on Sunday that "Israel rejects the 15-point document", the US-backed roadmap that pairs Hamas disarmament with a phased Israeli withdrawal from Gaza. He said the Israeli army "will not carry out any withdrawal until Hamas is genuinely disarmed" and insisted on removal of all weaponry, including lighter arms, rather than "fictitious disarmament". Hamas said in a Telegram statement later that day that it remains committed to fully implementing the plan, which Trump announced on July 30 through the US-led Board of Peace. Al Jazeera reports that since the nominal ceasefire began in mid-October, at least 1,258 people have been killed and 4,139 wounded in Gaza, with the overall toll since October 2023 at 73,386 killed.

## 2. Iran sets six conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz as Trump says US is only 'semi-negotiating'
*World & Geopolitics · TIME*

Iran's Supreme National Security Council issued six demands the United States must meet before Tehran allows free navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, including lifting the naval blockade, ending sanctions, releasing frozen assets and paying war damages. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said a separate agreement with Oman on new shipping routes is in its "final stages" but does not by itself mean the Strait will reopen. Trump told Axios that Washington is "only semi-negotiating" with Tehran and is watching Iran's inflation and finances deteriorate under the blockade. Commodities firm Kpler estimates an average of 13 vessels a day now cross the Strait, against roughly 138 in a 24-hour period before the war began on February 28.

## 3. Intel announces proposed $15 billion common stock offering
*Business & Markets · Intel Newsroom*

Intel disclosed a proposed $15 billion underwritten public offering of common stock, with a 30-day option for underwriters to buy up to an additional $2.25 billion of shares at the offering price less underwriting discounts. The company said net proceeds will go to general corporate purposes including capital expenditures and working capital, citing customer signals of strong and sustainable demand driven by investment in AI compute. Intel pointed to physical AI, purpose-built silicon, advanced packaging and external wafers as growth areas. J.P. Morgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and Citigroup are acting as joint book-running managers; Intel shares fell in trading after the announcement on dilution concerns.

## 4. TSMC July revenue jumps 44.7% to a record on AI chip demand
*Business & Markets · CNBC*

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. reported July revenue of NT$467.58 billion, a record and an increase of about 44.7% from a year earlier. Cumulative revenue for January through July reached roughly NT$2.87 trillion, up about 37% on the same period in 2025. TSMC has said it expects 2026 revenue to grow slightly above 40% in US dollar terms, an outlook it raised after second-quarter earnings. As the contract manufacturer for Nvidia, Apple, AMD and Qualcomm, TSMC's monthly sales are one of the closest real-time reads on global AI infrastructure spending.

## 5. Meta releases Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model that runs on one GPU
*AI & Tech · Tech Startups*

Meta Platforms released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight AI model under an Apache 2.0 license that can run on a single consumer GPU. Distilled from Meta's larger Muse Spark series, it is aimed at always-on local agent workflows including coding, function calling, file organization and multi-step reasoning with failure recovery, and is available on Hugging Face for deployment via tools such as llama.cpp and Ollama. Meta says quantization reduces memory requirements enough for offline use without cloud dependency. CEO Mark Zuckerberg paired the release with a public push for open-weight AI, criticizing closed-model developers and defending distillation techniques.

## 6. Study of 20,070 patients links very high Lp(a) to higher stroke and cardiovascular death risk
*Science · ScienceDaily (Society for Cardiovascular Angiography and Interventions)*

Researchers analyzing stored plasma samples from 20,070 participants aged 40 and older in the NIH-funded ACCORD, PEACE and SPRINT trials found that an inherited cholesterol particle, lipoprotein(a), independently predicts cardiovascular risk. Over a median follow-up of 3.98 years, 1,461 participants (7.3%) had a major adverse cardiovascular event; an Lp(a) level of 175 nmol/L or higher was associated with increased risk of MACE (HR 1.31), cardiovascular death (HR 1.49) and stroke (HR 1.64), but not of heart attack. About one in five people has elevated Lp(a), and levels are largely genetically determined and typically produce no symptoms. The late-breaking findings were presented at the Society for Cardiovascular Angiography & Interventions 2026 Scientific Sessions in Montreal.

## 7. Total solar eclipse to cross Europe on August 12, with ESA broadcasting live from Spain
*Science · ScienceDaily (European Space Agency)*

A total solar eclipse will sweep across Greenland, Iceland, a small part of northeastern Portugal and Spain on August 12, 2026, with the rest of Europe seeing a partial eclipse. The European Space Agency will livestream the event from the Observatorio Astrofisico de Javalambre in Teruel, Spain, beginning at 19:30 CEST, with additional feeds from Leon and Fromista inside the path of totality. Javalambre will experience 1 minute and 21 seconds of totality at 20:31 CEST, near sunset. The broadcast, hosted by Maggie Aderin, will include telescope views, drone footage and commentary from ESA scientists.
