## 1. Ukraine says sea drones sank Russian warship at Libyan naval base
*World & Geopolitics · NPR (AP)*

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Monday that Ukrainian sea drones struck the Russian navy corvette Merkury at the Benghazi Naval Base in eastern Libya on Sunday, roughly 2,400 kilometers from the front line in Ukraine; satellite imagery showed the vessel capsized. Ukraine's Security Service said the operation also hit the sanctioned Russian cargo ship MV Angara and that a Ka-27 helicopter aboard the corvette went down with it. Libyan officials said at least 10 people, including Libyan and Russian nationals, were wounded. Eastern Libya's government has asked the UN for an international investigation into whether western Libyan territory was used to prepare the attack.

## 2. Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO; shares jump nearly 30%
*Business & Markets · CBS News*

Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO on Monday, less than two weeks after he resigned amid a scandal over an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence agencies suspected of spying for China, which China has denied. The move reverses board chair Harvey C. Jones's statement on last week's earnings call that the company did not intend to bring Huang back; Colette Kress had served as interim CEO. Nvidia shares rose nearly 30% after Monday's open, and the Nasdaq Composite erased its losses in early trading following a steep sell-off on Friday. President Trump welcomed the decision and said the U.S. government has no reason to believe Huang's return poses a national security risk.

## 3. Tajikistan notifies UN it is leaving the International Criminal Court
*World & Geopolitics · Meduza*

Tajikistan has formally notified the United Nations that it is withdrawing from the Rome Statute and leaving the International Criminal Court, according to Russia's TASS news agency. The notification was sent to UN Secretary-General António Guterres on November 20, the day Russian President Vladimir Putin, who has faced an ICC arrest warrant since 2023, visited the country without being detained. Under the Rome Statute, the withdrawal takes effect one year after notification. Reuters reported that Tajikistan is the fourth country to withdraw from the court since a U.S. diplomatic push.

## 4. Meta's chief AI officer warns superintelligence is 'arriving'
*AI & Tech · Al Jazeera*

Meta Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang warned on Sunday that superintelligence far exceeding human intellect is imminent and that humanity is unlikely to control AI capable of unconstrained recursive self-improvement. His post followed a claim by an Anthropic researcher that he had used the company's Claude model to prove the Riemann hypothesis, one of mathematics' most famous open problems; Wang said the milestone showed emerging models will be "clearly superhuman" at improving themselves. He called for urgent progress on safety, alignment and security and said society should not accept a "handover" to AI. The remarks stand out because Meta and CEO Mark Zuckerberg have rejected calls from other AI leaders for a coordinated slowdown in development.

## 5. Newly found viruses may be a missing link in the evolution of cellular compartments
*Science · ScienceDaily (University of Miami)*

University of Miami researchers have identified a previously unknown group of viruses with traits of both viruses and encapsulins, small protein compartments that enclose biochemical reactions inside bacteria and archaea. The study, published in Nature Communications, challenges the conventional view that encapsulins simply evolved from viruses and suggests the two may have repeatedly evolved from one another. Both share a protein structure known as the HK97-fold, and the team found the new viruses by mining databases of billions of environmental DNA sequences using bioinformatics, protein language models and AlphaFold. The researchers say the findings could inform biotechnology and research into the early evolution of life.

## 6. Spain's Supreme Court applies amnesty to Puigdemont, closing Catalan independence case
*World & Geopolitics · El Mundo*

Spain's Supreme Court on Monday applied the amnesty law to former Catalan president Carles Puigdemont and definitively closed the criminal case over the 2017 independence push, nine years after it was opened. The ruling also extinguishes potential criminal liability for former regional ministers Antoni Comín and Lluís Puig. The court rejected objections from the Vox party, which had argued a related question was still pending before the EU Court of Justice, and ordered a dismissal that cannot be reopened. Puigdemont, who left for Belgium in 2017, never appeared before a Spanish judge in the case; the decision follows a Constitutional Court ruling requiring the amnesty to cover embezzlement charges.
