## 1. Ukraine strikes Novorossiysk, Russia's last major Black Sea Fleet stronghold
*World & Geopolitics · NBC News*

Ukrainian forces struck the Russian naval base at Novorossiysk overnight on Wednesday using Palianytsia drone-missiles, Neptune cruise missiles and maritime unmanned systems, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said. He reported hits on air-defence positions, piers and seaport infrastructure at the base, which lies more than 300km from the front line. Russia moved most of its Black Sea Fleet vessels to Novorossiysk after earlier Ukrainian strikes on Sevastopol in occupied Crimea, making it the fleet's last major sanctuary. Ukrainian drones also targeted Anapa, Gelendzhik and the Temryuk district of Krasnodar region during the same overnight wave.

## 3. Syrian court sentences Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia for crimes against humanity
*World & Geopolitics · France 24*

The Fourth Criminal Court in Damascus sentenced ousted Syrian president Bashar al-Assad and his brother Maher to death in absentia on Tuesday for crimes against humanity and war crimes committed during Syria's 14-year conflict. Assad's maternal cousin Atef Najib, convicted of overseeing the 2011 crackdown in Daraa that helped trigger the uprising, was also sentenced to death and, unlike the Assad brothers, was present in court. The Assads fled to Russia after the government's collapse in December 2024 and were granted political asylum; Syria's new authorities have asked Moscow to hand them over. These are the first sentences handed down against Assad or members of his inner circle since the family's five decades in power ended.

## 4. US consumer inflation eases to 3.4% in July as energy prices fall
*Business & Markets · U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics*

The Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers rose 0.1% in July on a seasonally adjusted basis after falling 0.4% in June, and was up 3.4% over the last 12 months, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported. Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.2% on the month and 2.5% over the year, down from 2.6% in June. The energy index fell 1.5% in July as gasoline prices declined 2.9%, though energy remains up 14.7% year over year and gasoline up 24.6%. Shelter, which rose 0.1%, accounted for roughly two-thirds of the monthly all-items increase; the next CPI release is scheduled for September 11.

## 5. Sony and TSMC sign definitive agreement for image-sensor joint venture in Japan
*AI & Tech · Sony Semiconductor Solutions*

Sony Semiconductor Solutions and TSMC announced a legally binding definitive agreement to establish Advanced Vision Semiconductor Manufacturing Corporation in Koshi City, Kumamoto Prefecture, Japan. Sony will contribute approximately 465 billion yen through cash and the transfer of its newly built Kumamoto fab, while TSMC will contribute about 282 billion yen in cash, with the two totalling roughly $4.7bn. The venture will develop and manufacture next-generation image sensors for smartphones and is expected to begin volume production in 2029. Sony will be the sole controlling shareholder and the venture will operate as a consolidated subsidiary of Sony Group, subject to regulatory approvals.

## 6. Total solar eclipse crosses Iceland and northern Spain, Europe's first in 27 years
*Science · RTE*

Millions of people in Iceland and northern Spain gathered on Wednesday to watch mainland Europe's first total solar eclipse since 1999. Spanish authorities expected up to 6 million visitors in mostly rural areas along the path of totality across northern Spain and the Balearic Islands, while Iceland, a country of roughly 400,000 people, anticipated up to 80,000 visitors. Totality lasted up to 2 minutes and 13 seconds at its longest, reaching Iceland in the afternoon and Spain in the evening shortly before sunset. It was Iceland's first total eclipse since 1954 and the first over Reykjavik in almost 600 years; Spanish officials urged viewers to avoid areas where a heatwave had raised wildfire risk.

## 7. Ebola deaths in DR Congo pass 2,000 in fastest-growing outbreak on record
*Science · UN News*

Local officials in the Democratic Republic of the Congo reported on Tuesday that deaths from the current Ebola outbreak have surpassed 2,000, with half of that total recorded in the previous 20 days. With more than 4,000 confirmed cases, it is both the second-largest and the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record, behind only the 2014-2016 West Africa epidemic. The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo species, for which there are no approved vaccines or treatments; vaccine trials have been authorised in Canada and the UK, with results likely several months away. The World Health Organization says 60 to 70 percent of deaths occur in communities rather than treatment centres, and the response is hampered by armed violence in eastern DRC, including attacks on at least a dozen Ebola treatment facilities.
