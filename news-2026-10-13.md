## 1. Zelenskyy accuses Russia of violating Trump's 'energy ceasefire'
*World & Geopolitics · Al Jazeera*

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia violated the energy ceasefire US President Donald Trump announced on 11 October, speaking by video at the Warsaw Security Forum on 12 October. Russia attacked Naftogaz gas production facilities in the Kharkiv region overnight, causing a fire. The EU separately added almost 1,600 new sanctions on Russia's military industrial complex, which foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas called the largest expansion of its sanctions list since the war began. The dispute matters because both countries have been striking each other's energy infrastructure for months, adding upward pressure on global energy prices.

## 3. Nobel economics prize awarded to Robert Hall and Michael Woodford
*Business & Markets · Al Jazeera*

The Royal Swedish Academy awarded the 2026 Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences to US academics Robert Hall and Michael Woodford on 12 October. The prize recognizes macroeconomic models showing how monetary and fiscal policy can help stabilise economic fluctuations. Committee chair John Hassler said the work is now used by central banks and governments around the world and helps policymakers handle shocks such as energy crises. The prize is worth $1.2 million and was the last of this year's Nobel awards.

## 4. Award-winning microscopy image disqualified over AI use
*AI & Tech · Nature*

Nikon disqualified the first-place entry in its Small World in Motion microscopy competition on 9 October, saying it did not comply with rules stating that AI-generated videos are not permitted. The winning video, by Ning Xu of the National University of Singapore, showed cilia moving in the lungs; scientists on LinkedIn questioned the plausibility of some features after the 15 September award. Xu said his team used an AI model in post-processing to enhance regions below the cilia without making anatomical claims. Nikon said the decision concerned eligibility only, not Xu's reputation or intent, and the case highlights how AI-processed imagery is testing rules in scientific competitions.

## 5. Hidden signals in sleep can predict future health
*Science · Nature*

A Nature News & Views piece by Rachel K. Rowe of the University of Colorado Boulder discusses two studies, in Nature Communications and Nature Medicine, that use AI foundation models on sleep data. The models find physiological patterns in a single night of sleep that are linked to disease and mortality and that conventional sleep measures miss. Changes in sleep can emerge before a disease is diagnosed, suggesting sleep holds early signals of future health. The commentary says sleep data appear to contain substantially more health information than clinicians and researchers currently extract.
