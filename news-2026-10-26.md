## 1. US intelligence chief warns Iran has stepped up plots against American officials abroad
*World & Geopolitics · ABC News*

An unclassified Oct. 23 memo from Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to Secretary of State Marco Rubio says Iran has "substantially increased" efforts to target U.S. officials abroad, with lethal plotting reaching "advanced stages" in the Middle East and elsewhere. A U.S. official confirmed the memo is authentic; Axios first reported it. The warning comes as talks to end the war that began with U.S.-Israeli strikes in February have stalled despite a June memorandum of understanding, and as the UKMTO reported a tanker hit by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on Oct. 22. On Oct. 25, Saudi Arabia issued civil-defense safety warnings for Riyadh, Khamis Mushait and Abha amid Houthi cross-border attacks, and said the danger had passed shortly afterward.

## 2. Russian attacks kill 8 and injure 51 across Ukraine in a day
*World & Geopolitics · The Kyiv Independent*

Ukrainian authorities reported 8 people killed and 51 injured over the past day (Oct. 24-25). A drone struck a five-story residential building in Vinnytsia overnight, injuring six people and destroying four apartments. Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 153 Shahed-type drones, 92 of them jet-powered, plus Banderol cruise missiles, and that 119 drones were intercepted. Casualties were also reported in the Dnipropetrovsk, Kharkiv, Donetsk, Sumy, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regions.

## 4. Experts warn AI-found software flaws will cause more outages like this week's PHP disruptions
*AI & Tech · Reuters (via Honolulu Star-Advertiser)*

Seven cybersecurity professionals told Reuters that AI-driven discovery of software vulnerabilities will almost certainly cause more outages like those that hit government websites worldwide after weaknesses were found in PHP on Wednesday. VulnCheck research due in the coming days says AI helped double the number of flaws discovered year over year. Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince said he knows of "a bunch of additional severe vulnerabilities" in other critical software, while curl lead developer Daniel Stenberg argued AI-found bugs tend to be obscure and hard to exploit. Experts agreed that defenders' patching timelines are shrinking, but disagreed on whether widespread easy hacking will follow.

## 5. Bennu asteroid samples point to dust mixing near the water-ice line 4.5 billion years ago
*Science · ScienceDaily (ETH Zurich press release)*

A study in Science Advances led by ETH Zurich measured iron, titanium and chromium isotopes in samples returned by NASA's OSIRIS-REx mission. Bennu, asteroid Ryugu and CI meteorites share strikingly similar isotopic fingerprints that differ from other known asteroids, meteorite groups and planets. The authors propose these bodies formed near the water-ice line in the young Sun's disk, with Jupiter acting as a partial barrier to larger particles while fine dust circulated. The Jupiter-driven scenario is a proposal rather than a directly demonstrated result.

## 6. NYT: Sanctioned Russian gas firm Novatek secretly funded Alaska LNG project
*Business & Markets · The Kyiv Independent (citing The New York Times)*

The New York Times reported on Oct. 23 that Novatek, Russia's second-largest LNG producer and under U.S. sanctions since 2022, secretly funded Polar LNG, a company seeking to export Alaskan natural gas, which may violate sanctions. Former Polar LNG CEO John Riddle resigned in September after learning of the ties and alerted Trump administration officials; he said he had been told the money came from a UAE company. Novatek was also negotiating to sell technology to the project as part of a proposed U.S.-Russia business deal that envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner discussed with Putin in September. Novatek confirmed it sought "minority shareholder" status; it is unclear whether the envoys knew of the financial ties.
